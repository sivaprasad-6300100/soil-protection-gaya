About the project

A Community Service Project (CSP) is part of our B.Tech course under APSCHE. Each student works as a volunteer in a village or community for two months (180 hours).

My topic is Soil Protection. I wanted villagers, mainly farmers, to understand why soil must be saved, how to farm in a sustainable way, and what organic farming can do for them.

What I wanted to achieve

  • Find out how much farmers know about protecting soil.
  • See what soil problems are common in the local fields.
  • Learn how many farmers already use sustainable methods.
  • Encourage compost, crop rotation and less chemical fertilizer.
  • Suggest simple ways to make the soil more fertile.

Why soil matters

Soil is one of the most valuable natural resources we have. Farming depends on it, plants and animals live on it, and it controls water and stores nutrients.

Soil erosion

Erosion happens when wind and water carry away the top layer of soil, the part full of nutrients. Without it the land becomes weak and can slowly turn into desert. In Week 2 I read about the causes, looked at pictures, and then went to the fields to see erosion myself.

How erosion can be reduced

  • Stop erosion: contour ploughing, terrace farming, cover crops, keeping plants on the land, and not overgrazing.
  • Stop land damage: avoid cutting forests, misuse of land and too much irrigation (it makes soil salty), and plan land use carefully.
  • Control pollution: keep industrial waste out of soil, use fewer pesticides and chemical fertilizers, and prefer organic farming.

Eight-week project journey

Week by week, only what is written in my report. Where the report has nothing, I have said so.

    Soil testing

    Week 4 (21/06/26 to 26/06/26) was about soil testing. I first read about how it is done, then visited fields to see the procedure and collected different soil samples.

    I did simple physical tests on texture, colour and structure. I kept the soils side by side, watched how they changed to judge their quality, and wrote down my observations. My main learning: every soil has different properties.

    These were physical tests only. I did not do any lab tests, so there are no nutrient values here.

    Mini garden

    The mini garden near my house was the practical part of my project. I started it in Week 3, practised in it again in Week 5, and did the full process in Week 6.

    • I used natural materials like buffalo and cow dung to help stop soil erosion.
    • I added natural compost to the soil.
    • I planted vegetables and flowers to see how the plants and the soil did.

    Farmer awareness

    Farmer interactions

    In Week 1 I talked to farmers to learn what problems affect their soil. In Week 5 I went back to the fields to tell farmers how to protect it.

    Awareness sessions

    We used charts, posters and demonstrations to show the harm of chemical fertilizers and the benefits of compost, mulching, crop rotation and cow dung manure. We also played Telugu videos on soil damage.

    Compost demonstration

    We showed how to make a compost pit with cow dung, kitchen waste, dry leaves and crop waste.

    School session

    We held an interactive session at the local ZP High School so students could learn about soil health and tell their families.

    Group discussions

    In small group meetings we talked with farmers about mixed cropping.

    One small step

    We asked every villager to start at least one sustainable practice, like a compost pit or natural manure on a part of their land.

    Farmer survey

    Sample size: 20 farmers, selected randomly. We used field visits, direct interviews, observation and a questionnaire.

    Aware that excessive chemical fertilizer damages soil fertilityAbout 70%
    Practised soil testing before cultivation40%

    Other findings

    • Farmers who used organic compost said their yield and soil texture were better.
    • Few farmers knew about contour ploughing and crop rotation, but this improved after our awareness sessions.
    • Many people in the village became interested in soil conservation once they heard its long-term benefits.
    • Students and young people were keen to join plantation and awareness activities.

      Conclusion and recommendations

      Soil supports all life on Earth, so protecting it is necessary for farming, nature and a stable environment. Through this project I understood why soil is important, what threatens it, and how we can conserve it.

      Plant trees

      Keep plants and trees growing on the land.

      Reduce chemicals

      Use less chemical fertilizer and pesticide, and test the soil first.

      Farm organically

      Use compost, mulch, crop rotation and natural manure.

      Prevent erosion

      Use contour ploughing, terraces and cover crops.

      Keep teaching

      Keep running awareness sessions for farmers, schools and youth.

      Start small

      Every family can begin with one practice, such as a compost pit.
